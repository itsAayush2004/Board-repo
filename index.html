Responsive UI

One UI that reshapes itself for every screen.

Both ARTHIS sites are built to change layout with the screen, not just shrink. Pick a size to see the real current build.

ARTHIS.space

Under 600 px: one full-width portrait card (10.5 : 16), the search bar shrinks to an icon that opens a full-screen list, one rounded bottom bar, and the prompt reads “Tap to play”. Heavy 3D games ask to open in Game Screen instead of running inside the feed, and only the games either side of you stay loaded.

600–899 px: the card turns landscape (27 : 14), the header shows the full search bar from 768 px, and the bottom bar splits into a name pill and actions. Still one column: ads arrive as a pop-up after a minute instead of a side rail.

900 px and up: a collapsible games rail appears on the left, sized so its tiles stay square; an ad rail joins on the right from 1100 px, and from 1280 px the bottom bar becomes one centred bar. The hint changes to “Press space for next game”.

On low-end phones, Data Saver or 2G, games never start by themselves (a poster and a Play button instead) and the moving background drops from about 260 shapes to 140 on touch screens and 45 on lite devices.

ARTHIS.land

Under 720 px: the nav pill scales down and its labels turn into icons, the minimap shrinks, the compass moves to the bottom-right and the wall rests zoomed out (0.72 instead of 1.25) so more rooms fit. A room opens as a bottom sheet with a drag handle instead of a side panel. On the About page, buttons go full width and the feature grid drops to one column.

720 px and up: full text nav, wheel zoom, rooms that lift on hover and a side panel for room details. The About page runs a 4-column room grid and 3-column features, stepping down to 2 at 1024 px.

On touch screens the wall swaps to smaller tile images, draws locked rooms as plain plots and skips hover effects and blur.

This page adapts too

The board you are scrolling picks a level of detail for your device and steps down on its own if the frame rate drops.

  • Desktop: full 3D, soft shadows, sharp 2× rendering, items sway
  • Tablet: lighter shadows and 1.5× rendering
  • Phone or low-memory device: no shadows, no anti-aliasing, still items, and the board only redraws when something moves
  • No WebGL or Data Saver: a simple flat page with the images inline; the Simple view button in the header does the same

HexaBed

A procedural hex-grid terrain engine in Unity, plus editor tooling so a designer can build a world without opening a script.

  • 160+ hex tiles generated into one playable world with varied heights
  • Road generator picks straight, turn, ramp or pit pieces from neighbouring tile heights
  • HexTileEditor inspector with 60° snapping and GLB export (25+ world meshes)
